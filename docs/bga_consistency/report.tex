\title{\bfseries A consistency proof for \BGA{} carried out inside grounded
       arithmetic in Isabelle/Pure}
\author{Omar Muhammad}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Grounded arithmetic admits arbitrary recursive definitions by weakening
inference rules with \emph{habeas quid} termination premises.  The existing
mechanized consistency results for grounded arithmetic are carried out in
Isabelle/HOL, that is, in a classical metatheory far stronger than the object
logic.  This report documents a development that carries the argument out
inside a version of grounded arithmetic itself.  \QGA, an axiomatization of grounded
arithmetic with quantifiers, is embedded into
Isabelle/Pure in \texttt{GD.thy}.  Basic grounded arithmetic \BGA{} is
arithmetized on top of it in \texttt{BGA\_on\_GA.thy}: terms, formulas,
judgments, proofs, the operational semantics and the proof checker are encoded
as \QGA{} natural numbers, and the checker is proved sound with respect to the
encoded semantics under a small set of abstract assumptions about the encoding.
A concrete Cantor-pairing encoding in \texttt{encode.thy} discharges those
assumptions and yields, as a theorem of \QGA: for any two numbers
coding \BGA{} terms $a$ and $b$, no numbers $p_1$ and $p_2$ code \BGA{} proofs, from
empty hypotheses, of $a = b$ and of $a \neq b$ respectively.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The three layers}

The development consists of three theories, each importing the previous one.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Theory} & \textbf{Imports} & \textbf{Contributes}\\
\midrule
\texttt{GD.thy}          & \texttt{Pure}        & \QGA, an embedding of grounded arithmetic\\
                         &                      & in Isabelle/Pure\\[2pt]
\texttt{BGA\_on\_GA.thy} & \texttt{GD}          & \BGA{} arithmetized in \QGA, and the\\
                         &                      & soundness proof for its proof checker\\[2pt]
\texttt{encode.thy}      & \texttt{BGA\_on\_GA} & a concrete encoding discharging every\\
                         &                      & assumption, and the consistency theorem\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\texttt{BGA\_on\_GA.thy} does the heavy lifting.  It never commits to a
particular G\"odel numbering.  It fixes an interface (\isa{tag\_T},
\isa{load\_T}, \isa{pack\_T} and their formula counterparts) together with the
smallest set of assumptions under which the soundness argument goes through,
and proves everything relative to that interface.  \texttt{encode.thy} then
pays the arithmetic cost once, by exhibiting one encoding that satisfies the
assumptions, and instantiates the result.

\subsection{What is proved}

The final theorem, \isa{BGA\_syntactically\_consistent}, says that for any two
numbers coding \BGA{} terms $a$ and $b$, no numbers $p_1$ and $p_2$ code \BGA{}
proofs, from empty hypotheses, of $a = b$ and of $a \neq b$ respectively.

\QGA{} is stronger than \BGA, so this is a consistency proof of a weaker system
inside a stronger one, in the ordinary G\"odelian arrangement.  Its interest lies
elsewhere.  It shows that grounded arithmetic can carry a full metamathematical
argument about a formal system with no classical ambient logic available at any
point.  Turning it into a self-consistency result requires a reduction of \QGA{}
back to \BGA, which is the subject of \S\ref{sec:reduction} and is left for later
work.

\subsection{The two levels}
\label{sec:levels}

Two logics are in play throughout, and separating them is worth doing before any
rules appear.

\QGA{} is the logic under study and that we will use for proof writing.  Its formulas are the terms of type \isa{o}.
Its vocabulary, given in full in \S\ref{sec:qga}, consists of the primitives
\isa{∨} and \isa{¬}, the connectives \isa{∧}, \isa{⟶} and \isa{⟷} defined from
them, the equality \isa{=}, and the quantifiers \isa{∀} and \isa{∃}.

Isabelle/Pure is the framework in which \QGA{} is written down.  Pure is a small
calculus whose purpose is to state the rules of other logics, and it has three
symbols.  \isa{A ⟹ B} says that from a derivation of $A$ one obtains a derivation
of $B$, so a rule ``from $A$ infer $B$'' is written this way.  \isa{⋀x. A} says
that $A$ was derived for an arbitrary $x$, which is how the eigenvariable
condition of a rule is expressed: the premise \isa{⋀x. x N ⟹ Q x} of
\isa{forallI} records that \isa{Q} holds of a variable about which nothing was
assumed.  \isa{a ≡ b} is the definitional equality of the framework, used where a
\QGA{} notion is abbreviated rather than axiomatized.\footnote{%
  Definitional equality identifies terms that differ only in ways the logic
  cannot see: renaming a bound variable ($\alpha$), applying a lambda to its
  argument ($\beta$), and replacing \isa{λx. f x} by \isa{f} ($\eta$).  Unfolding
  a \isa{definition} is also an \isa{≡} step.}

The type system keeps the two levels apart.  \isa{o} is the type of \QGA{}
formulas and \isa{prop} the type of Pure judgments, connected by
\isa{Trueprop :: o ⇒ prop}, which is declared with the \isa{judgment} command and
printed invisibly.  Nothing maps \isa{prop} back to \isa{o}.  A \QGA{} formula can
therefore be asserted as a judgment, while \isa{⟹} and \isa{⋀} can never appear
inside a \QGA{} formula.

Pure holds hypothetical judgments freely, because \isa{⟹} may always be
introduced.  Converting one into a \QGA{} formula requires \isa{implI}, whose
premise \isa{a B} demands that the antecedent be decided.  \emph{Habeas quid} is
a gate between the two levels, and every implication occurring in a
\QGA{} formula has passed through it.

This arrangement is what supports the claim that the development is carried out
in \QGA{} rather than in Isabelle.  Pure's kernel offers assumption, introduction
and elimination for \isa{⟹} and \isa{⋀}, the rules for \isa{≡}, instantiation of
schematic variables, and citation of an axiom.  Only the last of these introduces
an assertion.  The others move an assertion between hypothesis and antecedent
position, replace a rule schema by one of its instances, or work on equalities.
Remove the axioms of \texttt{GD.thy} and Pure proves no assertion at all except
from an assumption of that same assertion, since to Pure the \QGA{} connectives
are constants about which it knows nothing.  Every \QGA{} formula established in
the development therefore comes from a \QGA{} rule. \omar{This is my hand wave for the claim. I want to do rigorous analysis + a consistency proof of QGA in HOL soon.}

One kernel rule needs care.  From \isa{A ≡ B} and \isa{A} one may infer \isa{B},
so an equality can introduce an assertion that appears nowhere in its premises.
Inside Pure, equalities come only from the conversions described in the footnote
above, none of which changes meaning.  They can also come from an axiom, and two
axioms of \texttt{GD.thy} are of that kind: \isa{eq\_reflection}
(\isa{x = y ⟹ x ≡ y}) and \isa{iff\_reflection} (\isa{p ⟷ q ⟹ p ≡ q}), which
exist so that Isabelle's simplifier can act as a derived rule.  These two are the
entire surface through which the framework could contribute strength.  Each
mirrors a \QGA{} rule together with congruence, \isa{eq\_reflection} mirroring
\isa{eqSubst}, whose context \isa{Q :: num ⇒ o} is already arbitrary, so both are
expected to be pretty tame. \omar{I want to do a rigorous analysis on this too.}

A last reliance on the framework concerns the quantifier syntax.  \QGA{}
formulas are the terms of type \isa{o}, and \isa{forall} has type
\isa{(num ⇒ o) ⇒ o}, so a quantified formula is \isa{forall} applied to a Pure
function.  Binding is therefore the framework's, and renaming, capture-avoiding
substitution and freshness come for free rather than being axiomatized.  This is
the usual higher-order abstract syntax treatment of a binder and is what keeps
\S\ref{sec:qga} free of a substitution calculus. \omar{ I think the minor concern here would be that the bodies one
may quantify over are the Pure terms of type \isa{num ⇒ o} over the \QGA{}
signature, so the formulas of \QGA{} are not given by an inductive definition.
So an arithmetization of \QGA{} must
represent quantifier bodies by a first-order syntax with explicit variables.}

In the natural-deduction presentations below, \QGA{} rules are written with
premises above the line and the conclusion below, with no explicit context.  A
premise discharged by a rule is written in brackets above the derivation that
uses it.  \BGA{} judgments carry an explicit list of hypotheses, so \BGA{} rules
are written as sequents $\Gamma \seq \varphi$.  All Isabelle snippets are
extracted verbatim from the theories by \texttt{extract.sh}, which replaces
Isabelle's ASCII escapes by the corresponding Unicode glyphs and changes nothing
else.

\section{Status}

The three theories are complete in the sense that \texttt{encode.thy}
discharges every assumption of \isa{bga\_fuller} by \isa{unfold\_locales} and
derives \isa{BGA\_syntactically\_consistent} from
\isa{conc2.syntactically\_consistent}.  What remains for this document is the
detail of the soundness proofs summarized in \S\ref{sec:proof}, and the content
of \S\ref{sec:reduction}.

Three points about the development itself are worth recording.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The concrete functions of \texttt{encode.thy}, the evaluator, the
      substitutions and the checkers, are introduced by an
      \isa{axiomatization} block whose axioms are their recursive \isa{:=}
      equations.  This is the native definitional mechanism of \GA{}
      (\S\ref{sec:defmech}), which is sound because \GA{} places no termination
      requirement on a recursive definition.  It does mean the functions are
      postulated to satisfy their equations rather than built by a conservative
      definitional package.
\item The exact evaluator \isa{eval} of \isa{bga\_semantics} is retained in the
      sources but is not on the critical path.  The consistency argument runs
      through the step-indexed \isa{eval\_fuel} and the relation \isa{evals}
      derived from it (\S\ref{sec:fuel}), for which the determinism lemma is
      available.
\item An earlier version of the development, the locale \isa{bga\_full} and its
      interpretation, is retained inside a comment block in
      \texttt{BGA\_on\_GA.thy} and is superseded by \isa{bga\_fuller}.  Nothing
      described here depends on it.
\end{enumerate}
