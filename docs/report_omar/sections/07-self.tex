\section{\QGA{} on \QGA: feasibility and the development skeleton}
\label{sec:self}

\providecommand{\tv}[1]{\ensuremath{\mathsf{#1}}}

\S\ref{sec:semantics} proposed a semantics for \QGA{} and listed six obligations
for a self-consistency proof, flagging item 3, internal completeness, as the
main risk.  This section reports on that plan.  The verdict is that the result
is reachable, that the plan as stated does not close, and that a change to one
clause of the semantics removes the obstruction that made it not close.  The
theories \texttt{pure/QGA\_on\_GA.thy} and \texttt{pure/qga\_encode.thy}
implement the revised plan: the locale tower, every definition, the abstract
consistency skeleton with its theorem proved in full, and the statement of
every remaining lemma.  They are in the \texttt{GD} session and check.

\subsection{Is it possible?}

Nothing in G\"odel's second theorem forbids it.  Both derivability conditions
that carry the proof fail here.  \QGA{} is not classical, so the diagonal
sentence is ungrounded rather than true-and-unprovable, and grounded truth is
recursively enumerable, which is what lets an effective system be complete for
its own semantics.  More sharply, the internal necessitation and distribution
steps that L\"ob's theorem needs are unavailable, because $\longrightarrow$
introduction carries a \emph{habeas quid} premise: from a derivation of $A$ one
does not get $\fn{Prov}(A) \longrightarrow \fn{Prov}(B)$ for free, since the
implication cannot be formed until its antecedent is known to be grounded.  Reflective
grounded arithmetic proves the corresponding facts --- soundness, completeness,
consistency --- for a system strictly stronger than \QGA, so the mathematics is
known to work out.  What is new here is only the metatheory in which it is
carried out.

Nor is the \emph{internal} carrying-out obviously blocked.  \texttt{BGA\_on\_GA}
already shows that \QGA{} can host a full metamathematical argument about a
formal system with no classical ambient logic.  The question is therefore not
whether grounded arithmetic can do metamathematics at all, but whether the
particular obligations that quantifiers add can be met inside it.

\subsection{Where the plan of \S\ref{sec:semantics} does not close}

The architecture of \S\ref{sec:proof} imposes one hard constraint, and it is
worth restating because it drives everything.  The soundness induction runs over
a proof list, \QGA{} induction instantiates a schematic predicate, so the
generalizations over the assignment, the judgment and the fuel must live inside
the object-level formula being inducted on, and the hypothesis-satisfaction
premise therefore sits in the antecedent of an object-level $\longrightarrow$.
Introducing that implication requires the antecedent to be grounded.  So:

\begin{center}
\emph{satisfaction of a hypothesis list, at a fixed index, must be decided.}
\end{center}

\noindent
That rules out the instance-wise reading of $\forall$, as \S\ref{sec:semantics:quant}
says, and forces the reflective one.  So far the report is right.

The difficulty appears one step later.  Under the reflective reading, three
facts that a classical development proves independently become entangled, and
they are entangled in a cycle:

\begin{itemize}
\item Soundness of $\forall$E needs the certificate found by the semantics to be
      sound, that is, it needs soundness.
\item Soundness of \textsc{exF} needs that no formula is both true and false,
      that is, it needs determinism.
\item Determinism at a universal needs to rule out a truth certificate colliding
      with a falsity witness, which again needs soundness.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Reflective grounded arithmetic hits the same cycle and breaks it in two steps:
first internal completeness, proved independently, then a \emph{witnessed}
variant of the evaluation relation whose quantifier clauses carry, as extra
positive premises, the semantic facts their certificates promise.  Determinism
and the $\forall$E case are immediate at the witnessed level, and the two
semantics are reconciled afterwards.

Transplanting that directly fails.  The witnessed clause for $\forall$ carries
``every numeral instance is true'', which is not decided at any fixed index, so
a witnessed satisfaction predicate cannot be the one sitting in the antecedent.

\subsection{The two changes that make it close}

Two changes to the semantics of \S\ref{sec:semantics}, both small, remove the
cycle.  Neither weakens the encoded system.

\paragraph{Split the semantics in two, and use each where it belongs.}
Decidedness is needed only for the \emph{hypotheses}; the conclusion of the
soundness statement may be as undecided as one likes, since $\longrightarrow$
introduction constrains only the antecedent.  So the development carries two
layers.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{official} & \textbf{witnessed}\\
\midrule
symbols   & \isa{evtt k f A}, \isa{evff k f A} & \isa{wsat f A}, \isa{wunsat f A}\\
kind      & total \QGA{} functions & \QGA{} recursive predicates\\
decided?  & yes, at every fixed \isa{k} & no, and it does not matter\\
occurs in & antecedents & conclusions\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The witnessed clauses are the official ones with the certificate's semantic
content added as a conjunct:
\begin{center}
\isa{wsat (∀v\_i. φ) A := (∃k. evtt k (∀v\_i. φ) A = 1) ∧ (∀n. wsat φ (asn\_put A i (S n)))}
\end{center}
Exclusivity, \isa{¬(wsat f A ∧ wunsat f A)}, then becomes a
plain strong induction on the formula code: a universal that is both carries
truth at every instance and falsity at one, so the collision reappears at a
strictly smaller code.  No appeal to soundness, so no cycle.  Soundness of
\textsc{exF} is then available, and soundness of $\forall$E reads its instance
straight off the clause instead of re-proving the certificate sound.

\paragraph{Keep truth and falsity as two separate searches.}
The obvious design makes formula evaluation a single three-valued function.
That forces a priority between the two quantifier searches, and whichever loses
can be preempted at a larger index, so monotonicity fails; recovering it needs
exclusivity, which needs soundness.  Two independent monotone searches
\isa{evtt} and \isa{evff} have no tie to break.  Exclusivity is simply not
claimed of them --- it is a theorem about the witnessed layer --- and the two
bridges \isa{wsat f A} $\Rightarrow$ \isa{sat f A} and \isa{wunsat f A}
$\Rightarrow$ \isa{unsat f A} then hold without either having to rule the other
out.

\subsection{The clause that removes the completeness obligation}

\S\ref{sec:semantics:quant} takes the certificate for $\forall x.\,\varphi$ to be
a proof of $x \N \seq \varphi$ with no residual hypotheses, and notes that the
$\forall$I case must then convert satisfied hypotheses back into derivations and
discharge them by \textsc{cut}, which is internal completeness, ``the main risk
in the plan''.  That obligation is an artefact of the clause, not of the design.
The development uses instead a \emph{context-relative} certificate.  A witness
$w = \langle j, \langle q, \Gamma\rangle\rangle$ is a tt-witness for
$\forall v_i.\,\varphi$ under $A$ when

\begin{center}
$q$ is a valid proof of $\;\Gamma,\, v_i \N \seq \varphi[A]$, \quad
$\Gamma$ is closed, \quad
$\Gamma$ is satisfied by $A$ within $j$ steps,
\end{center}

\noindent
where $\varphi[A]$ is the closure of $\varphi$ against $A$ sparing $v_i$.  Read
semantically this is ``the system proves the schematic instance from premises
that are themselves true here'' --- the original clause composed with the cut
that internal completeness was there to perform.  Its effect on the proof is
that $\forall$I no longer has to \emph{construct} a certificate: the certificate
is the suffix of the proof list the checker is already looking at, instantiated
by the closing substitution, which is one application of the \textsc{inst} rule.

Requiring $\Gamma$ to be closed is what keeps the clause stable under
substitution: a closed context's satisfaction does not depend on the assignment,
so the substitution lemma never has to move it.  Closing $\varphi$ against $A$ is
what keeps the two sides of the substitution lemma comparable; they differ only
in that one carries the closed term $a[A]$ where the other carries the numeral of
its value, and one \textsc{eqSubst} step converts between them once
$\seq a[A] = \bar{v}$ is available.

The net effect on the obligation list of \S\ref{sec:semantics} is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\S\ref{sec:semantics} obligation} & \textbf{status}\\
\midrule
1. arithmetization of \QGA's syntax and checker & unchanged, and the bulk of the work\\
2. fuelled evaluation, monotone, decided at each index & unchanged\\
3. internal completeness & \emph{removed}, except at terms (see below)\\
4. soundness of every rule & unchanged\\
5. determinism & \emph{now independent of soundness}\\
6. \fn{False} is false & unchanged, one evaluation\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Item 3 does not vanish entirely.  What survives is \emph{term} value
completeness, $t \Downarrow v \Rightarrow\; \seq t = \bar{v}$, which is needed
only to convert a certificate along the substitution lemma.  That is a
self-contained induction on the fuel inside the \BGA{} fragment, with one proof
list per term constructor.  It is a fraction of the formula-level completeness
the original plan required, and it does not interact with soundness.

\subsection{What the development contains}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Theory} & \textbf{Imports} & \textbf{Contributes}\\
\midrule
\texttt{QGA\_on\_GA.thy} & \texttt{GD} & \QGA{} arithmetized in \QGA: syntax,\\
                         &             & checker, both semantic layers,\\
                         &             & the soundness statement\\[2pt]
\texttt{qga\_encode.thy} & \texttt{QGA\_on\_GA} & one concrete encoding, the\\
                         &             & interpretation, the theorem\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The locale tower mirrors \texttt{BGA\_on\_GA.thy}:
\isa{qga\_suff\_syntax} $\subset$ \isa{qga\_suff\_semantics} $\subset$
\isa{qga\_consistent} for the abstract skeleton, then
\isa{qga\_encoding} $\subset$ \isa{qga\_subst} $\subset$ \isa{qga\_dfns}, then
one locale per rule group
(\isa{qga\_struct\_rule}, \isa{qga\_prop\_rule}, \isa{qga\_eq\_rule},
\isa{qga\_cond\_rule}, \isa{qga\_quant\_rule}, \isa{qga\_ind\_rule},
\isa{qga\_app\_rule}), merged into \isa{qga\_proof\_check}, then
\isa{qga\_fuel\_semantics} $\subset$ \isa{qga\_witnessed} $\subset$
\isa{qga\_full}, which is declared a sublocale of \isa{qga\_consistent}.

The encoded syntax is exactly the primitive vocabulary of \texttt{GD.thy}.
Terms are variables, $0$, $\Suc{\cdot}$, $\Pre{\cdot}$, the conditional and
definition application; the conditional's guard is a \emph{formula}, so term and
formula codes are mutually recursive, which is the one structural difference
from \BGA.  Formulas are $=$, $\neg$, $\vee$, $\forall$, $\exists$ and the
formula-level conditional; $\wedge$, $\longrightarrow$, $\longleftrightarrow$,
$\neq$, $\N$, $\B$, \fn{True} and \fn{False} are abbreviations here as they are
in \texttt{GD.thy}.

The Pure operators are translated as \S\ref{sec:levels} reads them.  A rule
\isa{⟦P; Q⟧ ⟹ R} becomes ``from $\Gamma \seq P$ and $\Gamma \seq Q$ infer
$\Gamma \seq R$''.  A premise \isa{P ⟹ R}, as in $\vee$E1, becomes
$P, \Gamma \seq R$: the hypothesis is discharged.  A premise
\isa{⋀x. x N ⟹ Q x}, as in $\forall$I and \textsc{Ind}, becomes
$v_i \N, \Gamma \seq Q[v_i]$ \emph{together with the eigenvariable condition}
that $v_i$ occurs neither in $\Gamma$ nor in the conclusion, discharged by
\isa{fresh\_H i (hyp\_of J)} and \isa{fresh\_F i (conc\_of J)} with $i$ taken to
be $J + 1$, since every variable index occurring in a judgment is below the
judgment's own code.  This is \BGA's device in \isa{check\_ind}, reused.  The
four operations Pure supplies for free --- \isa{assume}, $\Longrightarrow$
introduction and elimination, and instantiation of a schematic variable ---
become the four explicit rules \textsc{hyp}, \textsc{weak}, \textsc{cut} and
\textsc{inst}, since nothing supplies them to the encoded system.

One point about \textsc{inst} is worth recording, because it fixes the shape of
assignments.  Pure instantiates a schematic variable with an \emph{arbitrary}
term and incurs no \emph{habeas quid} obligation.  That is sound only if a free
variable of the encoded system may denote nothing, so an assignment maps an
index either to $\Suc{v}$, meaning the value $v$, or to $0$, meaning bottom.
Quantified variables, by contrast, range over values only, which is exactly what
the $x \N$ premises of $\forall$I, $\forall$E and $\exists$I enforce.  This is
the variable-range invariant of reflective grounded arithmetic, and adopting it
is what makes \textsc{inst} unconditional and the substitution lemma free of
side conditions.

Every rule is effective without numeric guessing, by one convention: when a
premise is determined by the conclusion the checker looks it up with \isa{mem},
and when a premise is larger than the conclusion the checker scans the proof
list and decomposes what it finds.  Only \textsc{inst} and the two template
rules --- \textsc{eqSubst} and the lazy conditional pair --- need a bounded
countdown, each bounded by the code of the judgment being justified, as
\isa{check\_template} is in \S\ref{sec:bga}.

\subsection{Status and what remains}

\isa{qga\_consistent.syntactically\_consistent} is proved in full: no formula is
both provable and refutable from no hypotheses.  Because \QGA{} has negation as
a primitive, this is the ordinary statement of consistency, and not the matched
pair $a = b$ / $a \neq b$ that \BGA's lack of negation forced in
\S\ref{sec:consistency}.  \texttt{qga\_encode.thy} discharges every structural
assumption of the interface by \isa{unfold\_locales} and \isa{fact}, and derives
\isa{QGA\_syntactically\_consistent}.

What is not yet proved is the arithmetic in between.  Forty-five lemmas in
\texttt{QGA\_on\_GA.thy} and one in \texttt{qga\_encode.thy} stand as \isa{sorry},
each with a comment naming the argument that discharges it.  They fall into four
groups, in increasing order of cost.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Groundedness and totality} (about twenty lemmas).  Every encoded
      function is total and every checker is boolean.  These are
      \isa{condTB} chains over decided guards under \isa{strong\_induction} or
      \isa{list\_induct}, and are the direct analogues of \isa{fresh\_T\_bool},
      \isa{eval\_fuel\_N} and \isa{proof\_is\_bool}.  Mechanical, and the
      largest group by line count.
\item \textbf{Monotonicity and freshness}.  \isa{evT\_mono}, \isa{evtt\_mono},
      \isa{evff\_mono} and the three \isa{\_fresh} lemmas.  \BGA's
      \isa{eval\_fuel\_add} argument, plus the observation that a witness
      carrying its own fuel makes the quantifier clauses monotone by
      construction.
\item \textbf{The substitution lemmas} for \isa{wsat} and \isa{wunsat}, and the
      two internal derivability lemmas \isa{term\_value\_complete} and
      \isa{closure\_by\_inst}.  These are the ones that needed the design work
      above, and they are the only place where the development constructs a
      proof code rather than consuming one.
\item \textbf{Soundness}, one case per rule.  The propositional and conditional
      cases read off the clauses, the equality cases go through the template
      lemma, \textsc{Ind} is a semantic induction, and the three interesting
      cases are the three discussed above.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
A realistic estimate, calibrated against \texttt{BGA\_on\_GA.thy} at twenty-three
thousand lines for six term constructors, two formula constructors and thirteen
rules, is that the finished \texttt{QGA\_on\_GA.thy} is three to five times that.
The scaling is in the rule count and the connective count, not in the difficulty:
after the two changes above, no step of the argument is harder than its \BGA{}
counterpart, and one step, determinism, is easier.

\subsection{What this would and would not settle}

It would settle that \QGA{} proves an arithmetized statement of its own
consistency.  The scepticism \S\ref{sec:semantics} anticipates remains
legitimate and is worth stating plainly: the development supplies no adequacy
statement relating the arithmetized syntax to the Pure-level formulas of
\S\ref{sec:levels}, so what is proved consistent is the encoded system, and the
claim that the encoded system \emph{is} \QGA{} rests on inspection of the tag
tables and the rule list rather than on a theorem.  The first-order encoding of
binding also makes the encoded system a shade different from the higher-order
abstract syntax of \texttt{GD.thy}, in the direction \S\ref{sec:levels} already
flagged.  \S\ref{sec:reduction} avoids both questions by a different route and
remains worth pursuing independently.
